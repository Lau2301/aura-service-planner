    Product thinking

    Why I'd build this, how I'd know it worked, and what I'd leave out.

    The problem (my assumption, to test with the service team)

    Taps report filter life, flavour and CO₂ levels and errors, but service is probably still planned on fixed cycles or after a customer complains. That means two kinds of waste: trips to taps that didn't need a visit yet, and taps that run empty before the next visit. Customers notice the second one.

    How I'd know it worked

    • Taps running empty before a visit: down 50%
    • Kilometres driven per visit: down 20%
    • Emergency call-outs: down
    • Agent accepted by technicians: 80% or more, with every rejection reason reviewed weekly
    • Guardrail: customer complaints about service must not go up

    What I would not build (yet)

    • Auto-sending customer messages. The agent drafts and a person sends, until trust is earned.
    • Full route optimisation across all technicians. It's a big build, and it only pays off once suggestions are trusted.
    • A chat interface. Technicians need a clear list, not a conversation.

    Rollout

    1. Shadow mode (2 weeks): the agent suggests, technicians plan as usual, and we compare the two. That tells us how often it would have been right, at no risk.
    2. One region: technicians accept or reject suggestions. We track the metrics above and fix the top rejection reasons.
    3. All regions: only once acceptance holds above 80% and complaints are flat.

    Questions I'd ask technicians in week one

    • How do you plan your week today, and what makes you change the plan?
    • What's the most annoying wasted trip you made last month?
    • Which tap readings do you trust, and which ones are often wrong?
    • What would make you ignore a suggestion from the agent?

    Why the agent shows its working

    My MSc thesis audited an AI model that screened startup founders. Its explanations sounded reasonable and almost never mentioned gender, yet its shortlists still held fewer women than the pools they came from. The lesson I took: an AI's explanation alone isn't proof. So here every suggestion links to the data behind it, every rejection is logged, and success is measured on outcomes, not on how convincing the agent sounds.
